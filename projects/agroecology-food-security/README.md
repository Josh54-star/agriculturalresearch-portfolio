# Agroecological Practices & Household Food Security

## Research portfolio project

### Overview

This project examines how combinations of agroecological practices may influence household food security and dietary outcomes among smallholder farming households in Kenya.

The project reflects my research interest in moving beyond the analysis of individual agricultural practices toward understanding **practice bundles, production diversity, and the pathways connecting farm management to household food security**.

### Research problem

Smallholder farmers frequently adopt multiple agricultural practices simultaneously. Evaluating practices one at a time can therefore miss complementarities, substitution effects, and the broader farm-system context in which adoption decisions occur.

The project investigates whether distinct bundles of agroecological practices can be identified and whether these bundles are associated with differences in household food security and dietary diversity.

### Core research questions

1. What combinations of agroecological practices are commonly adopted by smallholder farmers?
2. What farmer and farm characteristics are associated with membership in different practice bundles?
3. How are practice bundles associated with household dietary diversity and food security?
4. Does production diversity provide a plausible pathway linking agricultural practice bundles to household food-security outcomes?

### Practice domains

The analytical framework considers practice domains such as:

- Soil fertility enhancement
- Soil and water management
- Ecological and biodiversity management
- Pest and disease management
- Crop and livestock diversification

### Data and measurement

The intended survey structure includes binary or categorical measures of practice adoption together with household, farm, production, market-access, and food-security variables.

Potential outcome measures include:

- Household Dietary Diversity Score (HDDS)
- Food insecurity indicators
- Production diversity
- Farm income / market participation

### Analytical approach

The project is designed around a quantitative cross-sectional research framework, complemented where appropriate by qualitative evidence from key informant interviews and focus group discussions.

Planned quantitative analysis includes:

1. Descriptive statistics and adoption profiles
2. Identification of practice bundles using latent class analysis (LCA)
3. Multivariable modelling of bundle membership and outcomes
4. Mediation/pathway analysis to examine the role of production diversity
5. Robustness and model-diagnostic checks

### Reproducibility

The final analytical repository will contain documented code, variable definitions, data-processing steps, model specifications, and reproducible tables/figures where the underlying data can legally be shared.

Confidential or unpublished research data will not be uploaded. Where necessary, a synthetic or public demonstration dataset will be used to reproduce the analytical workflow.
